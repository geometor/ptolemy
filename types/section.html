Section 1 · Notes

Unlike the Almagest, no Greek manuscript of Ptolemy's Planisphaerium (Ἅπλωσις ἐπιφανείας σφαίρας) has survived. Translated into Arabic in ninth-century Baghdad and revised with astrolabe chapters around 1000 CE in Córdoba by Maslama al-Majrīṭī, the work entered Latin Europe when Hermann of Carinthia (Hermannus Secundus) completed his Latin version at Toulouse on 1 June 1143 (dedicated to Thierry of Chartres).

Section 3 · Notes

Euclid III.35 as an Inversive Invariant: Ptolemy’s reliance on Eucl. III.35 ($ae \cdot eg = he \cdot et$) illustrates how classical Greek geometers handled the power of a point with respect to a circle. In the modern theory of circle inversion (developed by Steiner, Möbius, and Plücker in the 19th century), this property is expressed by stating that the point $e$ has constant power $-R^2$ with respect to every circle representing an inclined great circle on the sphere.

Section 20 · General Conclusion on the Construction of the Planisphere

In this manner, therefore, it is fitting that the construction in the planisphere be made of the circles in the corporeal sphere—namely those found by reason of the equinoctial circle, those parallel to it, and also the meridian circles, as well as those found by reason of the zodiac, those parallel to it, and also the circles of the horizon. In such a construction, the pole of the equinoctial circle holds the place of the center both for the right circle itself and for all circles parallel to the right circle; which rule compels the northern ones always to be smaller and the southern ones always larger, the former decreasing as in the sphere, but the latter increasing in reverse order to the sphere, and likewise extending all meridians into straight lines. Yet the pole of the zodiac is center neither for itself nor for any parallel to it; whence it happens that one of them is without a center—that is, it becomes a straight line. In the great circles passing through this pole it is otherwise; for those passing through both poles become straight lines, on which the centers of the unequal parallels to the zodiac are located. Section 20 · Summary

Equatorial vs. Ecliptic Projection Symmetries: The equatorial pole acts as center for the equator and all its parallels (northern shrinking, southern expanding), while meridians project as concurrent straight lines. Conversely, the ecliptic pole is non-concentric with the zodiac and its parallels (one parallel becoming a straight line), and only the solstitial colure projects as a straight line.
