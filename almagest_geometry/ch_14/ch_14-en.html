Chapter 14 · On the Arcs Between the Equator and the Ecliptic¶

Almagest Book I, Chapter 14 (Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 76–79)¶

Now that this theorem has been set forth in advance, we shall make the first demonstration of the proposed arcs as follows.

Let the circle through both poles—those of the equator and those of the circle through the middle of the zodiacal signs—be \(AB\Gamma\Delta\), let the semicircle of the equator be \(AE\Gamma\), and let that of the circle through the middle of the signs be \(BE\Delta\), and let point \(E\) be their intersection at the vernal equinox, so that \(B\) is the winter solstice and \(\Delta\) the summer solstice. On arc \(AB\Gamma\) let there be taken

the pole of the equator \(AE\Gamma\), and let it be point \(Z\); let arc \(EH\) of the circle through the middle of the signs be cut off and supposed equal to \(30\) parts of those of which the great circle is \(360\); through \(Z\) and \(H\) let an arc of a great circle \(ZH\Theta\) be described, and let it be proposed, of course, to find \(H\Theta\). Let it be laid down in advance both here and generally in all similar demonstrations, so that we may not repeat ourselves on each occasion, that whenever we speak of the magnitudes of arcs or straight lines in terms of how many degrees or parts they are, for arcs we mean those of which the circumference of the great circle is \(360\) parts, and for straight lines those of which the diameter of the circle is \(120\).

Since, then, in a diagram of great circles, across the two arcs \(AZ\) and \(AE\) two arcs \(Z\Theta\) and \(EB\) have been drawn intersecting one another at \(H\), the ratio of the chord subtending double \(ZA\) to the chord subtending double \(AB\) is compounded of the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Theta Z\) to the chord subtending double \(\Theta H\) and the ratio of the chord subtending double \(HE\) to the chord subtending double \(EB\). Now double arc \(ZA\) is \(180^\circ\) and the chord subtending it is \(120^p\), while double \(AB\) according to our agreed ratio of \(83\) to \(11\) is \(47^\circ 42' 40''\), and the chord subtending it is \(48^p 31' 55''\); and again, double arc \(HE\)

is \(60^\circ\) and the chord subtending it is \(60^p\), while double \(EB\) is \(180^\circ\) and the chord subtending it is \(120^p\). If therefore from the ratio of \(120^p\) to \(48^p 31' 55''\) we remove the ratio of \(60^p\) to \(120^p\), there remains the ratio of the chord subtending double \(Z\Theta\) to the chord subtending double \(\Theta H\), namely that of \(120^p\) to \(24^p 15' 56''\). Now double arc \(Z\Theta\) is \(180^\circ\), and the chord subtending it is \(120^p\); therefore the chord subtending double \(\Theta H\) is \(24^p 15' 56''\) of the same parts. Consequently double arc \(\Theta H\) is \(23^\circ 19' 59''\), and \(\Theta H\) itself is \(11^\circ 40'\) of the same degrees approximately.

Again, let arc \(EH\) be supposed \(60^\circ\), so that, the rest remaining the same, double \(EH\) becomes \(120^\circ\) and the chord subtending it \(103^p 55' 23''\). If therefore again from the ratio of \(120^p\) to \(48^p 31' 55''\) we remove the ratio of \(103^p 55' 23''\) to \(120^p\), there will remain the ratio of the chord subtending double \(Z\Theta\) to the chord subtending double \(\Theta H\), namely that of \(120^p\) to \(42^p 1' 48''\). Now the chord subtending double \(Z\Theta\) is \(120^p\); consequently the chord subtending double \(\Theta H\) will be \(42^p 1' 48''\) of the same parts. Therefore double arc \(\Theta H\) is \(41^\circ 0' 18''\), and \(\Theta H\) itself is \(20^\circ 30' 9''\) of the same degrees. Which was to be proved.
