Chapter 10 · Notes¶

Historical & Mathematical Analysis¶

1. Ptolemy’s Single-Circle Pentagon vs. Euclid IV.11¶

In Euclid's Elements IV.11, inscribing a regular pentagon in a given circle proceeds indirectly: one first constructs an auxiliary golden isosceles triangle ($72^\circ\text{–}72^\circ\text{–}36^\circ$) outside the circle via Elements II.11 and IV.10, inscribes a similar triangle via IV.2, and bisects its base angles.

In Almagest I.10 (Heiberg 1898, pp. 32–33), Ptolemy replaces that multi-stage sequence with an extraordinarily economical direct construction inside the target circle itself, combining Eucl. II.6, Eucl. XIII.9, and Eucl. XIII.10:

  • Because $E$ bisects radius $\Delta\Gamma = R$, the right triangle $\triangle B\Delta E$ has legs $R$ and $\frac{R}{2}$, hence hypotenuse $EB = \frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}R$.

  • Swinging $EB$ onto the opposite radius $\Delta A$ produces $\Delta Z = EB - E\Delta = \frac{\sqrt{5} - 1}{2}R = \frac{R}{\phi}$, the exact regular decagon chord $s_{10} = 2R\sin 18^\circ$.

  • Simultaneously, the hypotenuse $BZ$ of right triangle $\triangle B\Delta Z$ combines $B\Delta = R = s_6$ and $\Delta Z = s_{10}$ at right angles, realizing Elements XIII.10 ($s_5^2 = s_6^2 + s_{10}^2$) in a single line segment $BZ$!

2. Equivalent Modern Trigonometric Identities¶

Translating Ptolemy’s chord function via $\operatorname{crd}(\theta) = 2R\sin(\theta/2) = 120\sin(\theta/2)$ and $\operatorname{crd}(180^\circ - \theta) = 120\cos(\theta/2)$ reveals that every foundational identity of plane trigonometry enters Western mathematics through Almagest I.10:

  • Supplementary Chord (p. 35): $\sin^2\frac{\theta}{2} + \cos^2\frac{\theta}{2} = 1$.

  • Chord Difference & Sum (pp. 38, 41): $\sin(\frac{\alpha}{2} \pm \frac{\beta}{2}) = \sin\frac{\alpha}{2}\cos\frac{\beta}{2} \pm \cos\frac{\alpha}{2}\sin\frac{\beta}{2}$.

  • Half-Arc Chord Lemma (p. 39): $\sin^2\frac{\theta}{4} = \frac{1 - \cos(\theta/2)}{2}$.

  • Ptolemy’s Inequality Lemma (p. 43): Strict concavity of $\sin x$ on $(0, \frac{\pi}{2})$, i.e. $\frac{\sin x}{x}$ strictly decreases so $\frac{\sin(\alpha/2)}{\sin(\beta/2)} < \frac{\alpha}{\beta}$ for $0 < \beta < \alpha < \pi$.

3. Note on $\operatorname{crd}(\frac{3}{4}^\circ) = 0;47,8^p$ vs. Exact $0;47,7,25^p$¶

Evaluating the exact SymPy radical expression for $\operatorname{crd}(\frac{3}{4}^\circ)$ in geometor.ptolemy.pentagon_chords.build_radical_chord_table() gives $0.78539398\dots = 0;47,7,25^p$ (which rounds to 0;47,7), whereas Heiberg’s Greek text (p. 40, l. 19) prints 0;47,8 (οʹ μζʹ ηʹʹ) because Ptolemy took the square root of the already-rounded sexagesimal difference $120 - \operatorname{crd}(178\frac{1}{2}^\circ)$ at two sexagesimal places. Crucially, whether one uses the exact radical $\frac{4}{3}\operatorname{crd}(\frac{3}{4}^\circ) = 1.04719007\dots = 1;2,50,23^p$ or Ptolemy’s rounded $\frac{4}{3}(0;47,8) = 1;2,50,40^p$, both round identically to 1;2,50!

4. Ptolemy’s $\pi \approx 3;8,30 = \frac{377}{120}$ and Archimedes’ Bounds¶

In Almagest VI.7 (Heiberg 1898, p. 513), Ptolemy explicitly states that the ratio of circumference to diameter is $3;8,30$, “for such a ratio lies very nearly halfway between $3\frac{1}{7}$ and $3\frac{10}{71}$, which Archimedes used.” Indeed, multiplying the $360$-gon perimeter $360 \cdot \operatorname{crd}(1^\circ) = 360 \cdot (1;2,50^p)$ and dividing by $D = 120^p$ gives:

$$\pi_{\text{Ptolemy}} = 3 \times \left(1 + \frac{2}{60} + \frac{50}{3600}\right) = 3;8,30 = \frac{377}{120} = 3.1416666\dots,$$

lying within $7.4 \times 10^{-5}$ of true $\pi = 3.14159265\dots$ and strictly inside Archimedes' interval $\left(\frac{223}{71}, \frac{22}{7}\right) \approx (3.140845, 3.142857)$ (Dimensio Circuli Prop. 3).

5. Pure-Rational Verification in geometor.ptolemy¶

In geometor.ptolemy.cyclic_quadrilateral.construct_ptolemy_quadrilateral(), Ptolemy’s auxiliary similarity point $E \in AC$ ($\angle ABE = \angle DBC$) is constructed by compass-and-straightedge rhombus reflection strictly over $\mathbb{Q}$ (avoiding extraneous $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})$ extensions). On the circle of diameter $65$ (preset=”pythagorean_65”), the four rational vertices yield integer sides $(25, 39, 60, 52)$, integer diagonals $(56, 63)$, and exact rational similarity division $AE = \frac{500}{21}, EC = \frac{676}{21}$, verifying $56 \times 63 = 3528 = 25 \times 60 + 39 \times 52$ in pure rational arithmetic.
