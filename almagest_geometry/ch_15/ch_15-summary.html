Chapter 15 · Summary¶

The 90-Degree Solar Declination Table¶

Chapter 15 (Heiberg I, pp. 80–81) presents the complete quadrant table mapping ecliptic longitude \(\lambda \in \{1^\circ, 2^\circ, \dots, 90^\circ\}\) (measured from the vernal or autumnal equinox) to the corresponding meridian arc of solar declination \(\delta(\lambda)\) between the celestial equator and the ecliptic:

\[\operatorname{crd}(2\delta) = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2\varepsilon)\,\operatorname{crd}(2\lambda)}{120}, \qquad \varepsilon = 23^\circ 51' 20''.\]

  • Quadrant Symmetry: Because the four quadrants of the ecliptic are symmetric about the equinoxes and solstices, the 90 entries suffice for the entire \(360^\circ\) zodiac.

  • Key Checkpoints: At \(\lambda = 30^\circ\) (end of Aries), \(\delta = 11^\circ 39' 59''\); at \(\lambda = 60^\circ\) (end of Taurus), \(\delta = 20^\circ 30' 9''\); and at \(\lambda = 90^\circ\) (solstice), \(\delta = \varepsilon = 23^\circ 51' 20''\).
