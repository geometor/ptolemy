Chapter 13 · Preliminaries for Spherical Demonstrations¶

Almagest Book I, Chapter 13 (Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 68–76)¶

Since it follows next to demonstrate the individual magnitudes of the arcs cut off between the equator and the circle through the middle of the zodiacal signs along great circles described through the poles of the equator, we shall first set out brief and useful lemmas, by means of which we shall carry out nearly all demonstrations concerning spherical matters as simply and methodically as possible.

Across two straight lines AB and AΓ, let there be drawn

f. 0079 · p. 69

two straight lines BE and ΓΔ, intersecting one another at point Z.

I say that the ratio of ΓA to \(AE\) is compounded of the ratio of ΓΔ to \(\Delta Z\) and the ratio of \(ZB\) to BE.

For through E let EH be drawn parallel to ΓΔ. Since ΓΔ and EH are parallel, the ratio of ΓA to \(EA\) is the same as that of ΓΔ to EH [Eucl. VI, 4]. Taking \(Z\Delta\) externally, the ratio of ΓΔ to EH will be compounded of the ratio of ΓΔ to \(\Delta Z\) and the ratio of \(\Delta Z\) to \(HE\); so that the ratio of ΓA to \(AE\) is also compounded of the ratio of ΓΔ to \(\Delta Z\) and that of \(\Delta Z\) to \(HE\). But the ratio of \(\Delta Z\) to \(HE\) is also the same as that of \(ZB\) to BE [Eucl. VI, 4], because EH and \(Z\Delta\) are again parallel; therefore the ratio of ΓA to \(AE\) is compounded of the ratio of ΓΔ to \(\Delta Z\) and that of \(ZB\) to BE; which was proposed to be proved.

\[\frac{\Gamma A}{AE} = \frac{\Gamma\Delta}{\Delta Z} \cdot \frac{ZB}{BE}\]

In the same way it will be shown also by division that the ratio of \(\Gamma E\) to \(EA\) is compounded of the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(\Delta Z\) and the ratio of \(\Delta B\) to \(BA\), when a line is drawn through A parallel to EB and \(\Gamma\Delta H\) is produced to meet it. For again, since

f. 0080 · p. 70

\(AH\) is parallel to \(EZ\), as \(\Gamma E\) is to \(EA\), so is \(\Gamma Z\) to \(ZH\) [Eucl. VI, 2]. But when \(Z\Delta\) is taken externally, the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(ZH\) is compounded of the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(Z\Delta\) and the ratio of \(\Delta Z\) to \(ZH\); and the ratio of \(\Delta Z\) to \(ZH\) is the same as that of \(\Delta B\) to \(BA\), because \(BA\) and \(ZH\) are drawn across the parallels \(AH\) and \(ZB\); therefore the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(ZH\) is compounded of the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(\Delta Z\) and that of \(\Delta B\) to \(BA\). But the ratio of \(\Gamma E\) to \(EA\) is the same as that of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(ZH\); and therefore the ratio of \(\Gamma E\) to \(EA\) is compounded of the ratio of \(\Gamma Z\) to \(\Delta Z\) and that of \(\Delta B\) to \(BA\); which was to be proved.

\[\frac{\Gamma E}{EA} = \frac{\Gamma Z}{\Delta Z} \cdot \frac{\Delta B}{BA}\]

Again, let there be a circle \(AB\Gamma\), whose center is \(\Delta\), and upon its circumference let three arbitrary points \(A\), \(B\), \(\Gamma\) be taken, such that each of the arcs \(AB\), \(B\Gamma\) is less than a semicircle; and let the same condition be understood for the arcs taken hereafter; and let \(A\Gamma\) and \(\Delta EB\) be joined.

f. 0081 · p. 71

I say that, as the chord subtending double the arc \(AB\) is to the chord subtending double the arc \(B\Gamma\), so is the straight line \(AE\) to the straight line \(E\Gamma\).

For let perpendiculars \(AZ\) and \(\Gamma H\) be drawn from points \(A\) and \(\Gamma\) to \(\Delta B\). Since \(AZ\) is parallel to \(\Gamma H\), and the straight line \(AE\Gamma\) is drawn across them, as \(AZ\) is to \(\Gamma H\), so is \(AE\) to \(E\Gamma\) [Eucl. VI, 4]. But the ratio of \(AZ\) to \(\Gamma H\) is the same as that of the chord subtending double the arc \(AB\) to the chord subtending double the arc \(B\Gamma\); for each is half of each; therefore the ratio of \(AE\) to \(E\Gamma\) is also the same as that of the chord subtending double \(AB\) to the chord subtending double \(B\Gamma\); which was to be proved.

\[\frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 AB)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 B\Gamma)} = \frac{AE}{E\Gamma}\]

From this it follows immediately that, even if the whole arc \(A\Gamma\) and the ratio of the chord subtending double \(AB\) to the chord subtending double \(B\Gamma\) are given, each of the arcs \(AB\) and \(B\Gamma\) will also be given. For with the same figure set out, let \(A\Delta\) be joined, and from \(\Delta\) let perpendicular \(\Delta Z\) be drawn to \(AE\Gamma\). Now that, when arc \(A\Gamma\) is given, angle \(A\Delta Z\)—

f. 0082 · p. 72

subtending half of it—will be given, and the whole triangle \(A\Delta Z\) as well, is clear; and since, when the whole straight line \(A\Gamma\) is given, the ratio of \(AE\) to \(E\Gamma\), being the same as that of the chord subtending double \(AB\) to the chord subtending double \(B\Gamma\), is also supposed given, both \(AE\) will be given [Eucl. Data 7] and the remainder \(ZE\). And for this reason, since \(\Delta Z\) is also given, both the angle \(E\Delta Z\) of the right-angled triangle \(E\Delta Z\) and the whole angle \(A\Delta B\) will be given; so that both the arc \(AB\) and the remainder \(B\Gamma\) will be given; which was to be proved.

Again, let there be a circle \(AB\Gamma\) about center \(\Delta\), and on its circumference let three points \(A\), \(B\), \(\Gamma\) be taken, such that each of the arcs \(AB\), \(A\Gamma\) is less than a semicircle; and let the same condition be understood for the arcs taken hereafter; and let \(\Delta A\) and \(\Gamma B\) be joined, produced, and meet at point \(E\).

f. 0083 · p. 73

I say that, as the chord subtending double the arc \(\Gamma A\) is to the chord subtending double the arc \(AB\), so is the straight line \(\Gamma E\) to \(BE\).

For similarly to the preceding lemma, if from \(B\) and \(\Gamma\) we draw perpendiculars \(BZ\) and \(\Gamma H\) to \(\Delta A\), then because they are parallel, as \(\Gamma H\) is to \(BZ\), so will \(\Gamma E\) be to \(EB\) [Eucl. VI, 4]; so that also, as the chord subtending double \(\Gamma A\) is to the chord subtending double \(AB\), so is \(\Gamma E\) to \(EB\); which was to be proved.

\[\frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Gamma A)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 AB)} = \frac{\Gamma E}{EB}\]

And here too it follows immediately that, even if arc \(\Gamma B\) alone is given, and the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma A\) to the chord subtending double \(AB\) is given, arc \(AB\) will also be given. For again, in the like figure, when \(\Delta B\) is joined and perpendicular \(\Delta Z\) is drawn to \(B\Gamma\), the

f. 0084 · p. 74

angle \(B\Delta Z\), subtending half of arc \(B\Gamma\), will be given; and therefore the whole right-angled triangle \(B\Delta Z\) is given. And since both the ratio of \(\Gamma E\) to \(EB\) is given and also the straight line \(\Gamma B\), both \(EB\) and the whole \(EBZ\) will be given; so that, since \(\Delta Z\) is also given, both the angle \(E\Delta Z\) of the same right-angled triangle and the remaining angle \(E\Delta B\) will be given. Consequently the arc \(AB\) will also be given.

These things having been premised, let arcs of great circles be drawn upon a spherical surface such that across two arcs AB and AΓ two drawn arcs BE and ΓΔ cut one another at point Z; and let each of them be less than a semicircle; and let the same be understood for all the figures.

I say then that the ratio of the chord subtending double the arc \(\Gamma E\) to the chord subtending double \(EA\) is compounded of the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma Z\) to the chord subtending double \(Z\Delta\) and the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Delta B\) to the chord subtending double \(BA\).

\[\frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Gamma E)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 EA)} = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Gamma Z)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 Z\Delta)} \cdot \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Delta B)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 BA)}\]

For let the center of the sphere be taken and let it be H, and from H to the intersections B, Z, E of the circles let \(HB\), HZ, and \(HE\) be drawn; and let \(A\Delta\) be joined, produced, and meet \(HB\), likewise produced, at point Θ, and similarly

f. 0085 · p. 75

let \(\Delta\Gamma\) and \(A\Gamma\), when joined, cut HZ and \(HE\) at points K and Λ. Then points Θ, K, Λ come to lie on one straight line because they lie simultaneously in two planes, both that of triangle \(A\Gamma\Delta\) and that of circle \(BZE\); and this line, when joined, produces across two straight lines \(\Theta A\) and \(\Gamma A\) the drawn lines ΘΛ and \(\Gamma\Delta\) cutting one another at point K; therefore the ratio of \(\Gamma\Lambda\) to \(\Lambda A\) is compounded of the ratio of \(\Gamma K\) to \(K\Delta\) and that of \(\Delta\Theta\) to \(\Theta A\) [p. 69, 21]. But as \(\Gamma\Lambda\) is to \(\Lambda A\), so is the chord subtending double \(\Gamma E\) to the chord subtending double arc \(EA\); and as \(\Gamma K\) is to \(K\Delta\), so is the chord subtending double arc \(\Gamma Z\) to the chord subtending double \(Z\Delta\) [p. 70, 17]; and as \(\Theta\Delta\) is to \(\Theta A\), so is the chord subtending double arc \(\Delta B\) to the chord subtending double \(BA\) [p. 72, 11]; and therefore the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma E\) to the chord subtending double \(EA\) is compounded of the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma Z\)

f. 0086 · p. 76

to the chord subtending double \(Z\Delta\) and the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Delta B\) to the chord subtending double \(BA\).

In the same way indeed, and just as in the planar figure of the straight lines [p. 68, 23], it is shown that the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma A\) to the chord subtending double \(EA\) is also compounded of the ratio of the chord subtending double \(\Gamma\Delta\) to the chord subtending double \(\Delta Z\) and that of the chord subtending double \(ZB\) to the chord subtending double \(BE\); which was proposed to be proved.

\[\frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Gamma A)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 EA)} = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Gamma\Delta)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Delta Z)} \cdot \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 ZB)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 BE)}\]
