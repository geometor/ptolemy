Section 16 · Drawing a Parallel to the Zodiac in the Planisphere¶

Planisphaerium, Section 16 (Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 252–254)¶

Now the description of parallels to the zodiac in the planisphere must be set forth. Therefore we describe the meridian circle passing through both poles abgd around center e, understanding line deb as the intelligible axis, point d as the south pole, aeg as the diameter of the equinoctial circle, and zht as the diameter of the circle parallel to the zodiac which we propose to describe in the planisphere. We therefore draw through point h a line parallel to line ag bounded by letters k and l, joining lines dmz, dcl, and dnt. I say that the circle whose diameter is zt can be designated about diameter mn. Its proof: for it will touch on both sides the two circles parallel to the equinoctial whose distance from it is in the quantity of arcs az and gt; and therefore we describe those two circles with the two lengths em and en; and it will also bisect the circle parallel to the equinoctial whose diameter is lk

at the meridian circle whose diameter is bd, which we describe at the length ce between points c, y, and f, which the circle described about mn will bisect, passing through points y and f, and will divide circle cy into two halves, just as happens in the corporeal sphere. Therefore straight lines will join b with z and b with q; and kl and dt will go straight forward until they meet at point r, which lastly will join with b. Since therefore the two angles bzd and bhq are right, it follows that points b, h, z, q lie upon the circumference of a circle; whence angle bqh must equal angle bzh, which equals angle bdt, since their bases are the same; thus angle bqr equals angle bdr, whence it is clear that points b, d, r, q lie upon the circumference of a circle. Therefore the rectangle contained by bh and hd equals that contained by rh and hq. But bh by hd equals the square on hl; therefore the square on hl equals

the rectangle contained by rh and hq. Moreover, rq is parallel to line nm; therefore the rectangle contained by em and en equals the square on ec. And since ec equals lines ey and ef, it follows that points n, y, m, f lie upon the circumference of a circle.
