Section 14 · Construction of Parallels to the Zodiac via the Equinoctial¶

Planisphaerium, Section 14 (Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 249–251)¶

The preceding part of this treatise covers circles parallel to the right circle as far as the rising of the signs; its continuation treats parallels to the zodiac, insofar as they determine the places of fixed stars, by which reason the instrument called the spider in the horoscoping instrument contains them. Therefore, from the circles described, we take the outermost one

f. 0460 · p. 250

containing all others within it, and describe it with letters a, b, g, d around center e in place of the meridian circles, its diameters cutting each other orthogonally being ag and bd. Having done this, we cut off from point d arc dz, whose quantity is at the measure of the distance from the equinoctial circle of a circle parallel to it described toward the south pole in the corporeal sphere. Then we draw a line from point g parallel to line ed, bounded by letters g and h; likewise a perpendicular ht descends from point h onto line ed, and g joins d, crossing line ht at point k. I say that if we cut off from line eg a segment equal to tk up to point l, and around center e at radius el describe circle clm, the distance of abgd from circle clm will be designated at the measure of an arc similar to arc dz. To make this plain, g joins m, cutting circle clm at point n; arc mn will be similar to arc dz, and so arc gz, the remainder of the quadrant of its circle, is similar to arc ln, the remainder of the quarter circle;

f. 0461 · p. 251

which may be shown clearly as follows. For as de is to line eg, so is dt to line tk. But de is equal to eg; therefore dt is also equal to tk. Yet tk equals em; therefore em equals td. Taking tm as a common middle segment, et will also equal md. Moreover, et was both equal and parallel to gh; so therefore md is also parallel and equal to the same gh; whence hd and gm must also be equal and parallel. Therefore angle gme equals angle zde; whence it follows that arc cln is similar to arc bgz, and thus also the remainder to the remainder of the semicircles—that is, mn is similar to that which is zd. Therefore, if circle clm be set as the equinoctial, circle abgd will be designated from it at the distance of arc ln similar to arc gz.
