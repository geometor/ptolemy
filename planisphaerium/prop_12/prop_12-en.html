Section 12 · Rising of the Signs in the Clime of Rhodes: First Sign¶

Planisphaerium, Section 12 (Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 246–247)¶

Next, therefore, in order to measure the rising of the signs in this clime, we shall likewise set up the equinoctial circle abgd around center e, and the zodiac hdzb. This done, we shall cut off from the zodiac arc bt, first to the measure of one sign—which is established to be Pisces— and join straight line tel, and likewise describe the horizon circle for the latitude of \(36^\circ\), as before, passing through points t and l and cutting the equinoctial at points m and n. And we shall draw line nem, and likewise from the horizon center c, placed as before, straight lines ce and ct, and lastly also the perpendicular to line tl, namely line cy.

Therefore, as was stated above, arc am is that difference by which Aries and Pisces each decrease in this clime from their rising in the right sphere, and the same by which each of the signs opposite them increases over its rising in the fixed sphere. Now it is established that line et, the semidiameter of the parallel circle designated at the beginning of Pisces, is \(73^p 39' 7''\) out of those parts of which \(82^p 35' 3''\) contain line ec, namely the distance of the centers

f. 0457 · p. 247

of the horizon and equinoctial.

Since, therefore, the excess of the square on tc over the square on ec is in \(3600^p\) parts, if that number be divided by line et and we pursue the rest in order as in the right sphere, we shall gather line ey as before as \(12^p 22' 12''\). Now out of those parts of which we count \(120^p\) in line ec opposite the right angle, line ey will have \(18^p\) parts and nearly one minute; the arc of whose chord is \(17^\circ 16'\) out of the \(360^\circ\) of the whole circle containing triangle ecy. Therefore, out of the \(360^\circ\) that we count in four right angles, angle ecy will have \(9^\circ 38'\); and since this is equal to angle tem, \(9^\circ 38'\) also measure arc am out of the \(360^\circ\) of the whole equinoctial circle.

Since, therefore, as was stated above, each of the four signs around the equinoctial points rises in the fixed sphere with \(27^\circ 50'\), when we subtract these \(9^\circ 38'\) from that sum, there will remain the number of the rising of Aries and of Pisces in this clime, namely \(19^\circ 12'\); whereas if we add those same \(9^\circ 38'\) to the sum placed above, there will accrue the number of the rising of Virgo and of Libra, namely \(36^\circ 28'\).
