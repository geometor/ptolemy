Section 18 · Parallels to the Zodiac Intersecting Perpetually Invisible Circles¶

Planisphaerium, Section 18 (Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 255–257)¶

Next, when a circle parallel to the zodiac—neither described in the planisphere nor yet marked on the sphere—has its portion in the invisible region cutting circles parallel to the right circle that are invisible near the south pole, whose distance from the zodiac is either less than the altitude of the head of Cancer in a definite place or less than the altitude of the head of Capricorn in a determinate place, we set the meridian circle abgd around center e. We shall therefore understand point d as the south pole, axis db, the diameter of the equinoctial circle ag, the diameter of the circle parallel to it that is never

f. 0466 · p. 256

visible as line zh, and the diameter of the circle cutting it among the parallels to the zodiac as line tkl. These being so placed, we draw semicircle zmh upon line zh and raise a line from point k to m parallel to line de. From which therefore, if we draw lines agn and dhn as well as dlc, the circle described at length en through points n, y, q will be one of the circles perpetually denied to the planisphere. And the circle described in place of the circle upon line tkl must pass through point c, cutting circle nyq into arcs similar to arcs hm and mz, since line km is

f. 0467 · p. 257

a common mean proportional to their surfaces. Let f therefore join with m, and at point e upon line ea let an angle be made equal to angle mfk, which is angle aey; whence the line produced to point y shows arc yq similar to arc mz. Thus the circle is designated in place of the circle upon line tkl parallel to the zodiac, whose distance from the equinoctial is in the quantity of arc gl, cutting the perpetually hidden parallels to the right circle with such similarity. This circle must be understood in the figure as passing through c and y to reach opposite point o, where dt and ea produced straight meet just as dh and eg meet at point n.
