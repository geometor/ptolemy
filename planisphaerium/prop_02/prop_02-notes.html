Section 2 · Notes¶

Astrolabe Plates (Tympana) & Horizon Geometry¶

  1. The Astrolabe Tympan: In classical and medieval astrolabes, removable plates (tympana or safā’ih) were engraved for specific terrestrial latitudes $\varphi$. The dominant circular arc on each plate is the local Horizon (circulus finitor), separating the visible upper hemisphere from the invisible lower hemisphere. Section 2 provides the mathematical proof that drawing the horizon circle through the East and West equatorial points and its meridian intersections accurately separates the spherical sky.

  2. Antipodal Points under Stereographic Inversion: Ptolemy’s relation $ze \cdot eh = ed^2 = R^2$ reveals that stereographic projection maps pairs of antipodal points on the celestial sphere onto collinear points inverse with respect to the equatorial circle in the plane. In modern complex analysis ($z \in \mathbb{C}$), if point $P$ corresponds to $z$, its antipodal point $-P$ corresponds to $-\frac{R^2}{\bar{z}}$, whose distances from the origin multiply to $R^2$.
