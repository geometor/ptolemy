Unit IV · Latin Diplomatic Lemmata (Heiberg 1907, pp. 227–234)
==============================================================

*Claudii Ptolemaei Planisphaerium* (trans. :person:`Hermann of Carinthia <hermann>`, Toulouse, 1143)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Structured diplomatic lemmata and geometric propositions keyed to :person:`J. L. Heiberg's <heiberg>` public-domain Teubner edition (:cite:`Opera Astronomica Minora, Vol. II, 1907, pp. 227–234 <heiberg:1907>`).

§1 · Principium Repraesentationis Planae (`Heiberg II, pp. 227–229`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

* **Exordium & Aequator (`p. 227, ll. 1–12`)**:
  :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>` opens to :person:`Syrus <syrus>` (*"Cum sit possibile, Jesure, et plerumque necessarium, ut in plano represententur circuli in speram corpoream incidentes..."*) by establishing a single foundational circle :g:`abgd <cABGD>` (*unius circuli positione, qui rectam speram determinat*) around center :g:`e <E>` representing the visible North Pole (*manifesti poli*), cut orthogonally by diameters :g:`ag <MK>` and :g:`bd <BD_ast>`.

* **Circuli Paralleli (`p. 227, ll. 13–24`)**:
  Every parallel circle on the sphere is described concentrically around :g:`e <E>` by visual rays drawn from the hidden South Pole (*ab occulto polo*): northern parallels toward the visible pole fall inside :g:`abgd <cABGD>` as circle :g:`tl <cTL>` (Cancer), whereas southern parallels toward the hidden pole fall outside :g:`abgd <cABGD>` as circle :g:`km <cKM>` (Capricorn).

* **Circulus Declivis / Signifer (`p. 228, ll. 14–28`)**:
  Bisecting segment `tm` at :g:`r <R>` and describing the eccentric circle :g:`mbtd <cMBTD>` tangent to Cancer at :g:`t <T>` and Capricorn at :g:`m <M>`, Ptolemy proves from the folded meridian rays :g:`dm <MD>` and :g:`dt <TD>` that the angle subtending the solstitial diameter is right (*"manifestum est angulum mdt rectum esse"*, :just:`Eucl. III.31 <III.31>`). Hence the circle on diameter `tm` passes strictly through the opposite equinoctial points :g:`b <B>` and :g:`d <D>`, bisecting the equator in the plane exactly as on the solid sphere.

§§2–3 · Circulus Finitor / Horizon (`Heiberg II, pp. 229–232`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

* **Sectio Aequatoris et Zodiaci (`pp. 229–232`)**:
  Every inclined great circle—and specifically the local horizon (*finitor*)—is represented by an eccentric circle whose diameter lies along the meridian and which bisects both the equatorial circle :g:`abgd <cABGD>` and the oblique zodiacal circle :g:`mbtd <cMBTD>` into equal semicircles of six zodiacal signs each.

§4 · Proportio Semidiametrorum Tropicarum (`Heiberg II, pp. 233–234`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

* **Geometric Inversion Proportion (`p. 233, ll. 1–24`)**:
  Comparing the similar right triangles formed by the stereographic rays from the pole across the equatorial radius :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, Ptolemy establishes the continuous geometric proportion between the winter tropic radius (`ez`), the equatorial radius (`ed`), and the summer tropic radius (`ek`):

  $$ez : ed = ed : ek \qquad (\textit{linee ez ad lineam ed atque ed ad lineam ek}).$$

* **Numerical Evaluation from the *Almagest* (`p. 234, ll. 5–27`)**:
  Taking the tropical obliquity $\varepsilon = 23^\circ 51' 20''$ (*partibus XXIII punctis LI secundis XX*) and the *Almagest* chords (*quemadmodum in Almagesti constitutum est*) $\operatorname{crd}(113^\circ 51' 20'') = 100;33,28^p$ and $\operatorname{crd}(66^\circ 8' 40'') = 65;29,0^p$ for an equatorial radius $ed = 60^p$ (*semidiametros circuli recti absolute LX partium*):

  * **Hyemalis Tropici Semidiametros (`ez`, Capricorn)**: *XCII puncta VIII secunde XV* ($92;8,15^p$).
  * **Aestivi Tropici Semidiametros (`ek`, Cancer)**: *partes XXXIX puncta IIII secunde XVIIII* ($39;4,19^p$).
  * **Totam Zodiaci Diametrum (`ez + ek`)**: *partes CXXXI puncta XII [secunde XXXIIII]* ($131;12,34^p$).
