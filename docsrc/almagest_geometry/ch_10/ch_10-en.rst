Chapter 10 · On the Magnitude of Chords in a Circle
===================================================

*Almagest* Book I, Chapter 10 (:cite:`Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 31–47 <heiberg:1898>`)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0041
   :page: p. 31

For ease of practical use, then, we shall hereafter make a tabular exposition of the magnitudes of these :term:`chords <eutheia-hypoteinousa>`, dividing the circumference into :math:`360` :term:`parts <moira>` and setting alongside the arcs increasing by half a degree the straight lines subtending them,
that is, how many :term:`parts <tmema>` they are when the diameter is divided into :math:`120` parts on account of the arithmetical convenience that will appear from the calculations themselves. First, however, we shall show how, by as few and the same theorems as possible, we may conduct a methodical and rapid approach to their magnitudes, so that we may not only

   :page: p. 32

.. picture:: 0042.g1.png
   :alt: Folio 0042 diagram (archival scan)

.. picture:: 0042.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0042 diagram (interactive vector)

possess the magnitudes of the chords set out without inspection, but may also readily manage their verification through their methodical geometric construction. In general, however, we shall employ numerical procedures after the :term:`sexagesimal <hexekoston>` manner because of the awkwardness of unit fractions,
and in multiplications and divisions we shall always aim at the nearest approximation, insofar as what is omitted differs by nothing significant from what is accurate to sense.

1. Decagon, Pentagon, Hexagon, Square, and Triangle (`Heiberg I, pp. 32–35`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig02_pentagon_decagon_construction.svg

First, let there be a semicircle :g:`ΑΒΓ <cABG>` upon diameter :g:`ΑΔΓ <AG>` around center :g:`Δ <D>`, and from :g:`Δ <D>` let :g:`ΔΒ <DB>` be drawn at right angles to :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`; let :g:`ΔΓ <EG>` be bisected at :g:`Ε <E>`, let :g:`ΕΒ <EB>` be joined, let :g:`ΕΖ <DZ>` be placed equal to it, and let :g:`ΖΒ <BZ>` be joined.
I say that :g:`ΖΔ <DZ>` is the side of the decagon, and :g:`ΒΖ <BZ>` of the pentagon. For since the straight line :g:`ΔΓ <EG>` has been bisected at :g:`Ε <E>`, and a certain straight line :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` is adjoined to it,

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0043
   :page: p. 33

the rectangle contained by :g:`ΓΖ <AG>` and :g:`ΖΔ <DZ>`, together with the square on :g:`ΕΔ <DE>`, is equal to the square on :g:`ΕΖ <DZ>` [:just:`Eucl. II, 6 <II.6>`], that is, to the square on :g:`ΒΕ <EB>`, since :g:`ΕΒ <EB>` is equal to :g:`ΖΕ <DZ>`.

.. math::

   \Gamma Z \cdot Z\Delta + E\Delta^2 = EZ^2 = BE^2

But the squares on :g:`ΕΔ <DE>` and :g:`ΔΒ <DB>` are equal to the square on :g:`ΕΒ <EB>` [:just:`Eucl. I, 47 <I.47>`]; therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`ΓΖ <AG>` and :g:`ΖΔ <DZ>`, together with the square on :g:`ΔΕ <DE>`, is equal to the squares on :g:`ΕΔ <DE>` and :g:`ΔΒ <DB>`.

.. math::

   \Gamma Z \cdot Z\Delta + E\Delta^2 = E\Delta^2 + \Delta B^2

And when the common square on :g:`ΕΔ <DE>` is subtracted, the remaining rectangle contained by :g:`ΓΖ <AG>` and :g:`ΖΔ <DZ>` is equal to the square on :g:`ΔΒ <DB>`, that is, to the square on :g:`ΔΓ <EG>`; therefore :g:`ΖΓ <AG>` has been cut in :term:`extreme and mean ratio <akron-kai-meson-logon>` at :g:`Δ <D>` [:just:`Eucl. VI, def. 3 <VI.def.3>`].

.. math::

   \Gamma Z \cdot Z\Delta = \Delta B^2 = \Delta\Gamma^2

Since then the side of the hexagon and the side of the decagon inscribed in the same circle, when placed on the same straight line, are cut in extreme and mean ratio [:just:`Eucl. XIII, 9 <XIII.9>`], and :g:`ΓΔ <EG>`, being a radius, contains the side of the hexagon [:just:`Eucl. IV, 15 coroll. <IV.15>`], :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` is therefore equal to the side of the decagon.
Similarly, since the side of the pentagon is in square equal to both the side of the hexagon and the side of the decagon of those inscribed in the same circle [:just:`Eucl. XIII, 10 <XIII.10>`], and in the right-angled triangle :g:`ΒΔΖ <triBDZ>`

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0044
   :page: p. 34

the square on :g:`ΒΖ <BZ>` is equal to both the square on :g:`ΒΔ <DB>`, which is the side of the hexagon, and the square on :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` [:just:`Eucl. I, 47 <I.47>`], which is the side of the decagon, :g:`ΒΖ <BZ>` is therefore equal to the side of the pentagon.

Since then, as I stated, we suppose the diameter of the circle to be :math:`120` parts, by what has been set forth :g:`ΔΕ <DE>`, being half of the radius, becomes :math:`30` parts and its square :math:`900`, while :g:`ΒΔ <DB>`, being a radius, is :math:`60` parts and its square :math:`3600`,
and the square on :g:`ΕΒ <EB>`, that is, the square on :g:`ΕΖ <DZ>`, becomes :math:`4500` in sum; in length, therefore, :g:`ΕΖ <DZ>` will be approximately :math:`67;4,55` parts, and the remainder :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` will be :math:`37;4,55` of the same parts.
Therefore the side of the decagon, subtending an arc of :math:`36` such parts as the circle is :math:`360`, will be :math:`37;4,55` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`. Again, since :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` is :math:`37;4,55` parts and its square :math:`1375;4,15`, and the square on :g:`ΔΒ <DB>` is :math:`3600` of the same,
which added together make the square on :g:`ΒΖ <BZ>` to be :math:`4975;4,15`, in length therefore

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0045
   :page: p. 35

:g:`ΒΖ <BZ>` will be approximately :math:`70;32,3` parts. And the side of the pentagon, therefore, subtending :math:`72` degrees of which the circle is :math:`360`, is :math:`70;32,3` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`.
It is obvious directly from this that the side of the hexagon, subtending :math:`60` degrees and being equal to the radius, is :math:`60` parts. Similarly, since the side of the square, subtending :math:`90` degrees, is in square double the radius, while the side of the triangle, subtending :math:`120` degrees, is in square triple the same,
and the square on the radius is :math:`3600` parts, the square on the side of the square will amount to :math:`7200`, and that on the side of the triangle to :math:`10800`.
So that in length also the straight line subtending :math:`90` degrees will be approximately :math:`84;51,10` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`, and that subtending :math:`120` degrees will be :math:`103;55,23` of the same.

.. math::

   \operatorname{crd}(90^\circ) \approx 84;51,10^p, \qquad \operatorname{crd}(120^\circ) \approx 103;55,23^p

Let these, then, be taken by us thus readily and by themselves, and it will be evident from this that when straight lines are given, the straight lines

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0046
   :page: p. 36

subtending the arcs remaining to complete the semicircle are also easily given, because the squares on them added together produce the square on the diameter.
For instance, since the straight line subtending :math:`36` degrees was shown to be :math:`37;4,55` parts and its square :math:`1375;4,15`, while the square on the diameter is :math:`14400` parts, the square on the chord subtending the remaining :math:`144` degrees to the semicircle will also be the remaining :math:`13024;55,45`,
and in length it will be approximately :math:`114;7,37` of the same parts, and similarly for the others.

In what manner the remaining particular chords will also be given from these, we shall show next after setting forth first a lemma very useful for the present treatise.

2. Cyclic Quadrilateral Lemma (`Heiberg I, pp. 36–37`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig03_ptolemy_cyclic_quadrilateral.svg

For let there be a circle having any inscribed :term:`quadrilateral <tetragonon-eggegrammenon>` :g:`ΑΒΓΔ <cABCD>`, and let :math:`A\Gamma` and :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` be joined. It must be proved that the rectangle contained by :math:`A\Gamma` and :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` is equal to both together, the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΔΓ <CD>` and the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΔ <DA>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BC>`.
For let angle :g:`ΑΒΕ <angABE>` be placed equal to angle :g:`ΔΒΓ <angDBC>`. If then we add the common angle :math:`EB\Delta`, angle :math:`AB\Delta` will also be equal to angle :math:`EB\Gamma`. And angle :math:`B\Delta A`

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0047
   :page: p. 37

.. picture:: 0047.g1.png
   :alt: Folio 0047 diagram (archival scan)

.. picture:: 0047.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0047 diagram (interactive vector)

is equal to angle :math:`B\Gamma E` [:just:`Eucl. III, 21 <III.21>`], for they subtend the same segment; therefore triangle :math:`AB\Delta` is equiangular with triangle :math:`B\Gamma E`. So that it is also proportional: as :g:`ΒΓ <BC>` is to :g:`ΓΕ <EC>`, so is :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` to :g:`ΔΑ <DA>` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 4 <VI.4>`];
therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΓ <BC>`, :g:`ΑΔ <DA>` is equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, :g:`ΓΕ <EC>` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 16 <VI.16>`].

.. math::

   B\Gamma \cdot A\Delta = B\Delta \cdot \Gamma E

Again, since angle :g:`ΑΒΕ <angABE>` is equal to angle :g:`ΔΒΓ <angDBC>`, and angle :math:`BAE` is equal to angle :math:`B\Delta\Gamma`, triangle :g:`ΑΒΕ <triABE>` is equiangular with triangle :g:`ΒΓΔ <triDBC>`;
it is therefore proportional: as :g:`ΒΑ <AB>` is to :g:`ΑΕ <AE>`, so is :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` to :g:`ΔΓ <CD>`; therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΑ <AB>`, :g:`ΔΓ <CD>` is equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, :g:`ΑΕ <AE>`. But it was proved that the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΓ <BC>`, :g:`ΑΔ <DA>` is equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, :g:`ΓΕ <EC>`;
and as a whole [:just:`Eucl. II, 1 <II.1>`], therefore, the rectangle contained by :math:`A\Gamma`, :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` is equal to both together, the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΔΓ <CD>` and the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΔ <DA>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BC>`, which was to be proved.

.. math::

   A\Gamma \cdot B\Delta = AB \cdot \Delta\Gamma + A\Delta \cdot B\Gamma

3. Chord of the Difference (`Heiberg I, pp. 37–39`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig04_chord_difference_and_sum.svg

This having been set forth beforehand, let there be a semicircle :g:`ΑΒΓΔ <semiDiff>` upon diameter :g:`ΑΔ <AD>`, and from :g:`Α <A>` let two

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0048
   :page: p. 38

.. picture:: 0048.g1.png
   :alt: Folio 0048 diagram (archival scan)

.. picture:: 0048.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0048 diagram (interactive vector)

chords :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΑΓ <AG>` be drawn through, and let each of them be given in magnitude, of such parts as the diameter is given as :math:`120`, and let :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` be joined.
I say that this also is given. For let :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` be joined; clearly these also are given because they are the chords remaining to complete the semicircle. Since then :g:`ΑΒΓΔ <quadDiff>` is a quadrilateral in a circle, the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` together with the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΔ <AD>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` is equal to the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`, :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`.
And both the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`, :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` and the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` are given; therefore the remaining rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΔ <AD>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` is given. And :g:`ΑΔ <AD>` is a diameter; therefore the straight line :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` is also given.

.. math::

   B\Gamma = \frac{A\Gamma \cdot B\Delta - AB \cdot \Gamma\Delta}{A\Delta}

And it has become clear to us that, if two arcs and the straight lines subtending them are given, the straight line subtending the difference of the two arcs will also be given. And it is plain that through this theorem we shall inscribe not a few other straight lines from the differences of those given by themselves,

4. Chord of the Half-Arc (`Heiberg I, pp. 39–41`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig05_half_arc_chord_lemma.svg

Again, let it be proposed, given a certain straight line in a circle, to find the straight line subtending half the subtended arc. Let there be a semicircle :g:`ΑΒΓ <semiABG>` upon diameter :g:`ΑΓ <AG>` and a given straight line :g:`ΓΒ <BG>`, and let arc :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` be bisected at :g:`Δ <D>`, and let :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΑΔ <AD>`, :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, :g:`ΔΓ <GD>` be joined,
and from :g:`Δ <D>` let :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` be drawn perpendicular to :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`. I say that :math:`Z\Gamma` is half of the excess of :g:`ΑΒ <AB>` and :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`. For let :g:`ΑΕ <AE>` be placed equal to :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, and let :g:`ΔΕ <DE>` be joined.
Since :g:`ΑΒ <AB>` is equal to :g:`ΑΕ <AE>`, and :g:`ΑΔ <AD>` is common, the two lines :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, :g:`ΑΔ <AD>` are equal to the two lines :g:`ΑΕ <AE>`, :g:`ΑΔ <AD>` each to each; and angle :math:`BA\Delta` is equal to angle :math:`EA\Delta` [:just:`Eucl. III, 27 <III.27>`]; therefore base :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` is equal to base :g:`ΔΕ <DE>` [:just:`Eucl. I, 4 <I.4>`].
But :g:`ΒΔ <BD>` is equal to :g:`ΔΓ <GD>`; therefore :g:`ΔΓ <GD>` also is equal to :g:`ΔΕ <DE>`. Since then, in the isosceles triangle :math:`\Delta E\Gamma`, from the vertex to the base a perpendicular

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0050
   :page: p. 40

:g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` has been drawn, :math:`EZ` is equal to :math:`Z\Gamma` [:just:`Eucl. I, 26 <I.26>`].
But the whole :math:`E\Gamma` is the excess of the straight lines :g:`ΑΒ <AB>` and :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`; therefore :math:`Z\Gamma` is half of their excess. So that, since when the straight line subtending arc :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` is posited, :g:`ΑΒ <AB>` remaining to complete the semicircle is immediately given,
:math:`Z\Gamma`, being half of the excess of :g:`ΑΓ <AG>` and :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, will also be given. Moreover, since in the right-angled triangle :math:`A\Gamma\Delta`, when perpendicular :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` is drawn, the right-angled triangle :math:`A\Delta\Gamma` becomes equiangular with :math:`\Delta\Gamma Z` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 8 <VI.8>`], and as :g:`ΑΓ <AG>` is to :g:`ΓΔ <GD>`, so is :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` to :math:`\Gamma Z`,
therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`, :math:`\Gamma Z` is equal to the square on :g:`ΓΔ <GD>`. And the rectangle contained by :g:`ΑΓ <AG>`, :math:`\Gamma Z` is given; therefore the square on :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` is also given. So that in length too the straight line :g:`ΓΔ <GD>` will be given, subtending half of arc :g:`ΒΓ <BG>`.

.. math::

   \Gamma\Delta^2 = A\Gamma \cdot \Gamma Z = \frac{1}{2}A\Gamma\,(A\Gamma - AB)

And through this theorem again very many others will be obtained at the halves of those already set forth, and in particular from the straight line subtending :math:`12` degrees, both that subtending :math:`6` and that subtending :math:`3` and that subtending one and a half and that subtending half and a quarter of one degree. And we find from the calculations

   :page: p. 41

.. picture:: 0051.g1.png
   :alt: Folio 0051 diagram (archival scan)

.. picture:: 0051.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0051 diagram (interactive vector)

that the straight line subtending one and a half degrees is approximately :math:`1;34,15` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`, and that subtending three-quarters of a degree is :math:`0;47,8` of the same.

5. Chord of the Sum (`Heiberg I, pp. 41–42`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

Again, let there be a circle :g:`ΑΒΓΔ <circSum>` with diameter :g:`ΑΔ <AD_sum>` and center :math:`Z`, and from :g:`Α <A_sum>` let two given arcs :g:`ΑΒ <AB_sum>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BG_sum>` be cut off in succession, and let the straight lines :g:`ΑΒ <AB_sum>`, :g:`ΒΓ <BG_sum>` subtending them be joined, these also being given. I say that if we join :g:`ΑΓ <AG_sum>`, it too will be given.
For let the diameter of the circle :math:`BZE` be drawn through :g:`Β <B_sum>`, and let :g:`ΒΔ <BD_sum>`, :g:`ΔΓ <GD_sum>`, :math:`\Gamma E`, :math:`\Delta E` be joined. It is clear at once that because of :g:`ΒΓ <BG_sum>`, :math:`\Gamma E` will also be given, and because of :g:`ΑΒ <AB_sum>`, both :g:`ΒΔ <BD_sum>` and :math:`\Delta E` will be given.
And for the same reasons as before, since :g:`ΒΓΔΕ <quadSum>` is a quadrilateral in a circle and :g:`ΒΔ <BD_sum>`, :math:`\Gamma E` are drawn across, the rectangle contained by the diagonals is equal to both together of the rectangles contained by the opposite sides;
so that, since the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΔ <BD_sum>`, :math:`\Gamma E` is given and the rectangle contained by :g:`ΒΓ <BG_sum>`, :math:`\Delta E` is given, the rectangle contained by :math:`BE`, :g:`ΓΔ <GD_sum>` is therefore also given.

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0052
   :page: p. 42

And the diameter :math:`BE` is also given, so the remaining straight line :g:`ΓΔ <GD_sum>` will be given, and on this account the chord remaining to complete the semicircle, :g:`ΓΑ <AG_sum>`, will also be given;
so that, if two arcs and the straight lines subtending them are given, the straight line subtending both arcs together by composition will also be given through this theorem.

.. math::

   \operatorname{crd}(\alpha + \beta) = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(\alpha)\operatorname{crd}(180^\circ - \beta) + \operatorname{crd}(\beta)\operatorname{crd}(180^\circ - \alpha)}{120}

Now it is evident that, by continually composing the chord subtending :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees with all those already set forth and computing the connected chords,
we shall directly inscribe all those which, when doubled, have a third part, and there will still remain only those lying between the intervals of :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees, two in each interval, since we make the inscription by half-degrees.
So that, if we find the straight line subtending the half-degree, this, by composition and difference with the given straight lines bounding the intervals, will also fill in all the remaining ones in between for us.
And since, when a certain straight line such as that subtending :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees is given, the straight line subtending the third part of the same arc is by no means given :term:`by means of lines <dia-ton-grammon>`—whereas if it were possible, we should immediately have also that subtending the half-degree—

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0053
   :page: p. 43

.. picture:: 0053.g1.png
   :alt: Folio 0053 diagram (archival scan)

.. picture:: 0053.g1.svg
   :alt: Folio 0053 diagram (interactive vector)

we shall first treat the chord subtending :math:`1` degree from both that subtending :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees and that subtending :math:`\frac{3}{4}` of a degree, laying down a lemma which, even if it cannot determine their magnitudes in general, yet for quantities so very small will be able to preserve agreement with those that are determinate.

6. Inequality Lemma and the Chord of One Degree (`Heiberg I, pp. 43–47`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig06_ptolemy_inequality_lemma.svg

For I say that, if two unequal straight lines are drawn across in a circle, the greater has to the less a lesser ratio than the arc upon the greater straight line has to the arc upon the less.

Let there be a circle :g:`ΑΒΓΔ <cABGD>`, and let two unequal straight lines be drawn in it, the less :g:`ΑΒ <AB>`, and the greater :g:`ΒΓ <BG>`.
I say that the straight line :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` has to the straight line :g:`ΒΑ <AB>` a lesser ratio than the arc :g:`ΒΓ <BG>` has to the arc :g:`ΒΑ <AB>`. For let angle :math:`AB\Gamma` be bisected by :g:`ΒΔ <BD>`, and let :g:`ΑΕΓ <AG>`, :math:`A\Delta`, and :g:`ΓΔ <DG>` be joined.
And since angle :math:`AB\Gamma` has been bisected by the straight line :g:`ΒΕΔ <BD>`, the straight line :g:`ΓΔ <DG>` is equal to

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0054
   :page: p. 44

:math:`A\Delta` [:just:`Eucl. III, 26, 29 <III.26>`], and :math:`\Gamma E` is greater than :math:`EA` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 3 <VI.3>`].
Now let :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>` be drawn from :g:`Δ <D>` perpendicular to :g:`ΑΕΓ <AG>`. Since then :math:`A\Delta` is greater than :math:`E\Delta`, and :math:`E\Delta` than :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>`, the circle described with center :g:`Δ <D>` and distance :math:`\Delta E` will cut :math:`A\Delta` and fall beyond :g:`ΔΖ <DZ>`. Let :g:`ΗΕΘ <arcHETheta>` be described, and let :math:`\Delta Z\Theta` be produced.
And since sector :math:`\Delta E\Theta` is greater than triangle :g:`ΔΕΖ <triDEZ>`, while triangle :g:`ΔΕΑ <triDAE>` is greater than sector :math:`\Delta EH`, therefore triangle :g:`ΔΕΖ <triDEZ>` has to triangle :g:`ΔΕΑ <triDAE>` a lesser ratio than sector :math:`\Delta E\Theta` has to :math:`\Delta EH`. But as triangle :g:`ΔΕΖ <triDEZ>` is to triangle :g:`ΔΕΑ <triDAE>`, so is straight line :math:`EZ` to :math:`EA` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 1 <VI.1>`],
and as sector :math:`\Delta E\Theta` is to sector :math:`\Delta EH`, so is angle :math:`Z\Delta E` to angle :math:`E\Delta A`; therefore straight line :math:`ZE` has to :math:`EA` a lesser ratio than angle :math:`Z\Delta E` has to angle :math:`E\Delta A`. And by composition, therefore, straight line :math:`ZA` has to :math:`EA` a lesser ratio than angle :math:`Z\Delta A` has to angle :math:`A\Delta E`;
and taking the doubles of the antecedents, straight line :g:`ΓΑ <AG>` has to :math:`AE` a lesser ratio than angle :math:`\Gamma\Delta A` has to

But as straight line :math:`\Gamma E` is to :math:`EA`, so is straight line :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` to :g:`ΒΑ <AB>` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 3 <VI.3>`], and as angle :math:`\Gamma\Delta B` is to angle :math:`B\Delta A`, so is arc :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` to :g:`ΒΑ <AB>` [:just:`Eucl. VI, 33 <VI.33>`]; therefore straight line :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` has to :g:`ΒΑ <AB>` a lesser ratio than arc :g:`ΓΒ <BG>` has to arc :g:`ΒΑ <AB>`.

.. math::

   \frac{\Gamma B}{BA} < \frac{\operatorname{arc}\Gamma B}{\operatorname{arc} BA}

With this, then, laid down, let there be a circle :math:`AB\Gamma`, and let two straight lines :math:`AB` and :math:`A\Gamma` be drawn in it; and first let :math:`AB` be supposed to subtend :math:`\frac{3}{4}` of one degree, and :math:`A\Gamma` :math:`1` degree. Since straight line :math:`A\Gamma` has to straight line :math:`BA` a lesser ratio than arc :math:`A\Gamma` has to :math:`AB`,
and arc :math:`A\Gamma` is one and one-third times :math:`AB`, straight line :math:`\Gamma A` is therefore less than one and one-third times :math:`BA`. But straight line :math:`AB` was shown to be :math:`0;47,8` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`; therefore straight line :math:`\Gamma A` is less than :math:`1;2,50` of the same, for these are approximately one and one-third times :math:`0;47,8`.

.. math::

   \operatorname{crd}(1^\circ) < \frac{4}{3}(0;47,8^p) = 1;2,50\tfrac{2}{3}^p

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0056
   :page: p. 46

Again, in the same figure let straight line :math:`AB` be supposed to subtend :math:`1` degree, and :math:`A\Gamma` :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees. In the same way, since arc :math:`A\Gamma` is one and a half times :math:`AB`, straight line :math:`\Gamma A` is therefore less than one and a half times :math:`BA`.
But we proved :math:`A\Gamma` to be :math:`1;34,15` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`; therefore straight line :math:`AB` is greater than :math:`1;2,50` of the same, for the aforementioned :math:`1;34,15` are one and a half times these. So that, since the straight line subtending one degree was shown to be both greater and less than the same numbers,
clearly we shall also have this approximately :math:`1;2,50` of such parts as the diameter is :math:`120`, and through what was previously proved also the chord subtending the half-degree, which is found to be approximately :math:`0;31,25` of the same.

.. math::

   \operatorname{crd}(1^\circ) \approx 1;2,50^p, \qquad \operatorname{crd}\left(\tfrac{1}{2}^\circ\right) \approx 0;31,25^p

And the remaining intervals will be filled in together, as we said, from the chord of the half-degree—for example, in the case of the first interval, that subtending :math:`2` degrees being shown by composition with :math:`1\frac{1}{2}` degrees, and from the excess relative to :math:`3` degrees, that subtending :math:`2\frac{1}{2}` degrees being given; and similarly for the rest.

So then the treatise on the straight lines in the circle might thus, I think, be most easily handled. In order that, as I said,

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0057
   :page: p. 47

we may have the magnitudes of the straight lines set out ready to hand for each of our needs, we shall subjoin tables of :math:`45` lines each for the sake of symmetry,
whose first columns will contain the magnitudes of the arcs increased by half-degree steps, whose second columns will contain the magnitudes of the straight lines placed alongside the arcs when the diameter is assumed to be :math:`120` parts, and whose third columns will contain the :math:`\frac{1}{30}` part of the increment of the straight lines for each half-degree,
so that, having also the mean increment for one sixtieth part—which differs not sensibly from exact truth—we may be able to calculate readily the magnitudes belonging even to the parts falling between the half-degrees. And it is easy to understand that, by means of the same theorems already set forth, even if we should fall into doubt regarding a scribal error
concerning any of the straight lines set beside the arcs in the table, we shall make both the examination and the correction easily, either from the chord subtending double the arc sought, or from its difference with certain other given chords, or from the straight line subtending the arc remaining to complete the semicircle.
