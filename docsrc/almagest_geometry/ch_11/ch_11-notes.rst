Chapter 11 · Notes
==================

Historical & Philological Analysis
----------------------------------

1. **Hipparchus vs. Ptolemy on Trigonometric Tables**:
   The earliest historical trigonometric table is attributed to :person:`Hipparchus <hipparchus>` (c. 140 BCE), who reportedly compiled a 12-book treatise on chords. Hipparchus tabulated chords in a circle of radius $R = 3438'$ at $7\frac{1}{2}^\circ$ increments. Ptolemy revolutionized chord calculation by reducing the interval from $7\frac{1}{2}^\circ$ to $0^\circ 30'$ (a fifteen-fold refinement!) and establishing the systematic sexagesimal radius $R = 60^p$.

2. **The Manuscript Tradition and Scribal Corrections**:
   Ptolemy's remarks in Chapter 11 on scribal error (`γραφικῆς ἁμαρτίας`) reflect the practical reality of ancient scriptoria. In Byzantine manuscripts such as *Vaticanus Graecus 1594*, marginal and interlinear corrections attest that readers repeatedly re-checked chord entries against Ptolemy's generative theorems (double arcs, differences, and supplements).

3. **Transmission to Indian and Islamic Astronomy**:
   Ptolemy's Table of Chords was transmitted to Sasanian Persia and India, where the chord function was halved to yield the sine function (*ardha-jya*, shortened to *jya*), translated into Arabic as *jayb* (pocket/fold), and into Latin by Gerard of Cremona as *sinus*. In Islamic astronomy, al-Battānī and Abū al-Wafā replaced chords entirely with sine and tangent tables.
