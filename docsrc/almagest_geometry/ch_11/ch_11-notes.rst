Chapter 11 · Notes
==================

Historical, Philological & Numerical Analysis
---------------------------------------------

1. **Primary Witness & Boundary**:
   Transcribed directly from :person:`J. L. Heiberg <heiberg>`, *Claudii Ptolemaei Opera quae exstant omnia*, Vol. I, Pars I (*Syntaxis Mathematica*, Leipzig: B. G. Teubner, 1898), pp. 48–63 (folios ``0058``–``0073``). The explanatory prose introducing the 45-line layout and scribal self-checking method stands at the close of Chapter 10 (p. 47, folio ``0057``).

2. **Greek Milesian Sexagesimal Numerals**:
   Heiberg prints Milesian alphabetic numerals throughout: :math:`\angle'` denotes :math:`\frac{1}{2}^\circ` (:math:`30'`), ``ο`` denotes zero (*ouden*), ``ϛ`` (*stigma*) denotes 6, and ``ϟ`` (*koppa*) denotes 90.

3. **Notable Printed Divergences & Manuscript Variants in Heiberg (1898)**:
   Across all 360 rows, Ptolemy's values match exact :math:`120 \sin(\theta/2)` to within :math:`\pm 1''` almost everywhere. Five notable entries in Heiberg's printed text depart further and reflect manuscript transmission recorded in his critical apparatus:

   - :math:`9^\circ 00'` (p. 48, l. 20): Heiberg prints ``9;24,51`` (``θ κδ να``); exact rounded value is ``9;24,54`` (preserved in MS ``D``, ``νδ``).
   - :math:`72^\circ 00'` (p. 54, l. 10): Heiberg prints ``70;32,04`` (``ο λβ δ``, following MS ``D``); Chapter 10 and MSS ``ABC`` give the exact rounded pentagon side ``70;32,03`` (``γ``).
   - :math:`88^\circ 30'` (p. 55, l. 43): Heiberg's text prints ``83;41,04`` (``πγ μα δ``), a scribal/typographical slip for ``83;44,04`` (``μδ``; confirmed by the surrounding entries ``83;21,33`` and ``84;06,32`` and by the apparatus note on MS ``D``).
   - :math:`97^\circ 00'` (p. 56, l. 15): Heiberg prints ``89;52,27`` (``πθ νβ κζ``); exact value is ``89;52,29`` (preserved in MS ``D``, ``κθ``).
   - :math:`118^\circ 30'` (p. 58, l. 13): Heiberg prints ``103;07,41`` (``ργ ζ μα``); exact value is ``103;07,44``.

4. **From Hipparchus to Sine Tables**:
   :person:`Hipparchus <hipparchus>` (ca. 140 BCE) reportedly tabulated chords at :math:`7\frac{1}{2}^\circ` steps in a circle of radius :math:`R = 3438'`. Ptolemy refined the grid fifteen-fold to :math:`0^\circ 30'` with sexagesimal radius :math:`R = 60^p`. In Indian and Islamic astronomy, halving the chord produced the half-chord (*ardha-jya* / *jya* → Arabic *jayb* → Latin *sinus*), directly bridging Ptolemy's table to modern trigonometry.
