Chapter 1 · Summary
===================

Philosophy, Mathematics, and the Aim of Astronomy
-------------------------------------------------

Chapter 1 (*Heiberg I, pp. 4–8*) opens the *Syntaxis Mathematica* with an epistemological preface addressed to :person:`Syrus <syrus>`, distinguishing theoretical from practical philosophy and adopting Aristotle's threefold division of theoretical science (*Metaphysics* E.1):

* **Theology**: Studies the invisible, unmoved first cause of cosmic motion, utterly separate from perceptible substance.
* **Physics**: Studies corruptible, ever-moving material quality below the lunar sphere.
* **Mathematics**: Mediates between both, investigating figure, number, magnitude, place, and time via indisputable arithmetic and geometric demonstration.

Because theology and physics yield conjecture, mathematics alone yields certain knowledge, illuminates divine order, informs physical motion, and cultivates moral proportion.
