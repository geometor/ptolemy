Chapter 12 · Notes
==================

Historical & Observational Analysis
-----------------------------------

1. **Eratosthenes, Hipparchus, and Ptolemy**:
   Ptolemy attributes the ratio $\frac{11}{83}$ to :person:`Eratosthenes <eratosthenes>` (c. 276–194 BCE) and notes that :person:`Hipparchus <hipparchus>` also adopted it. In decimal degrees, $\frac{11}{83} \times 360^\circ \approx 47.7108^\circ$, giving $\varepsilon \approx 23.8556^\circ = 23^\circ 51'20''$.

2. **Secular Variation of Earth's Obliquity**:
   Due to planetary gravitational perturbations (Milankovitch cycles), the obliquity of Earth's ecliptic decreases by approximately $46.8''$ per century according to Newcomb's formula:

   $$\varepsilon(T) = 23^\circ 27' 08.26'' - 46.845'' T - 0.00059'' T^2 + 0.00181'' T^3,$$

   where $T$ is in Julian centuries from J2000.0. Around 150 CE ($T \approx -18.5$), the true physical obliquity was approximately $23^\circ 41'$, meaning Ptolemy's value of $23^\circ 51'20''$ was slightly larger by about $10'$, partly reflecting refraction and solar parallax effects uncorrected in antiquity.

3. **Instrumental Heritage**:
   The vertical meridian quadrant plinth described by Ptolemy became the archetype of Islamic observatory mural quadrants (such as those constructed by al-Khujandī at Rayy and Ulugh Beg at Samarkand) and Tycho Brahe's famous mural quadrant at Uraniborg.
