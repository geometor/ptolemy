Section 7 · The Horizon Circle for the Clime of Rhodes
======================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 7 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, p. 236 <heiberg:1907>`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig09_planisphaerium_stereographic_astrolabe.svg

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0446
   :page: p. 236

And if we set each of the arcs :g:`gh` and :g:`gt` to :math:`54^\circ`, which is the distance from the equinoctial of the parallels touched by the horizon in the clime of Rhodes—which clime we take by way of example in the corporeal sphere—arc :g:`bt` will likewise be :math:`144^\circ`, its chord :math:`114^p 7' 37''`, arc :g:`bh` :math:`36^\circ`, and its chord :math:`37^p 4' 55''`.

Thus, therefore, since the ratio of :math:`114^p 7' 37''` to :math:`37^p 4' 55''` is the same as that of line :g:`ez` to line :g:`ed <BD_ast>` and of :g:`ed <BD_ast>` to line :g:`ek <EK>`, out of the :math:`60^p` parts making up line :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, line :g:`ez` will have :math:`184^p 39' 48''` and line :g:`ek <EK>` :math:`19^p 29' 42''`.

From these it is clear—since these two lines joined together make the diameter of the horizon we just mentioned, just as the tropic semidiameters make the diameter of the zodiac—that that diameter measures :math:`204^p 9' 30''` of those parts of which :math:`120^p` measure the diameter of the equinoctial; whence the semidiameter of the horizon must be :math:`102^p 4' 45''`, and the distance of its center from the equinoctial center :math:`82^p 35' 3''`.
