Section 7 · Notes
=================

The Rhodes Clime & Horizon Stereographic Identity
-------------------------------------------------

1. **The Fourth Clime (Rhodes)**:
   Following the standard Hellenistic geographical convention used in *Almagest* II.6 and II.8 (the parallel through Rhodes at :math:`\varphi = 36^\circ`, corresponding to a longest day of :math:`14\frac{1}{2}` equinoctial hours), Ptolemy adopts Rhodes as his primary worked example for oblique-sphere calculations in Sections 10–12.

2. **Exact Trigonometric Identity**:
   Notice that :math:`R_{\text{hor}} = \frac{1}{2}(R \tan(90^\circ - \varphi/2) + R \tan(\varphi/2)) = R \csc \varphi`, and :math:`d_{\text{hor}} = R \cot \varphi`. Thus :math:`R_{\text{hor}}^2 - d_{\text{hor}}^2 = R^2 = 3600`, confirming geometrically that the horizon bisects the equator circle in stereographic projection.
