Section 15 · Pole of the Zodiac and Great Circles Through the Poles
===================================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 15 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 251–252 <heiberg:1907>`)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0461
   :page: p. 251

Next it is fitting to pursue our purpose—namely, of designating those circles whose relation to the zodiac is like that of the circles already described to the equinoctial, so that the position of the stars may be evident to us
by their relation to this circle as well as to the equinoctial. For let there first be the equinoctial circle among the circles of the planisphere described with letters :g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`g`, :g:`d` around center :g:`e`, and the zodiac :g:`lbhd` around center :g:`k`,

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0462
   :page: p. 252

the straight line passing through both centers being :g:`lahg`,
and line :g:`bed` joining the intersections of the circles. Therefore we cut off arc :g:`bt` equal to the arc of distance between the pole of the equinoctial circle and the pole of the zodiac; the line will pass through :g:`dkt`, and point :g:`k` will in potentiality be the pole of the zodiac.
It is clear, therefore, that if this distance is determined by the fixed reckoning, a circle passing from this point through two points of the zodiac opposite across a diameter will also bisect the equinoctial circle;
for it is clear that every circle bisecting either of these necessarily bisects the other; and this will be the great circle bounding and cutting both orthogonally.
