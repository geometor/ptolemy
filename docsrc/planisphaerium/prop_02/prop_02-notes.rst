Section 2 · Notes
=================

Astrolabe Plates (*Tympana*) & Horizon Geometry
-----------------------------------------------

1. **The Astrolabe Tympan**:
   In classical and medieval astrolabes, removable plates (*tympana* or *safā'ih*) were engraved for specific terrestrial latitudes :math:`\varphi`. The dominant circular arc on each plate is the local **Horizon** (*circulus finitor*), separating the visible upper hemisphere from the invisible lower hemisphere. Section 2 provides the mathematical proof that drawing the horizon circle through the East and West equatorial points and its meridian intersections accurately separates the spherical sky.

2. **Antipodal Points under Stereographic Inversion**:
   Ptolemy's relation :math:`ze \cdot eh = ed^2 = R^2` reveals that stereographic projection maps pairs of antipodal points on the celestial sphere onto collinear points inverse with respect to the equatorial circle in the plane. In modern complex analysis (:math:`z \in \mathbb{C}`), if point :math:`P` corresponds to :math:`z`, its antipodal point :math:`-P` corresponds to :math:`-\frac{R^2}{\bar{z}}`, whose distances from the origin multiply to :math:`R^2`.
