Section 8 · Rising of the Signs in the Right Sphere: First Sign
===============================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 8 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 236–240 <heiberg:1907>`)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0446
   :page: p. 236

With these matters settled, it is next fitting to measure the quantity of the rising of the signs, as it occurs in the corporeal sphere. For let

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0447
   :page: p. 237

there be, as usual, the equinoctial circle :g:`abgd` around center :g:`e`, and the zodiac :g:`zbhd` around center :g:`t`,
with the diameters drawn orthogonally through :g:`e` in place of the meridian circle, one joining the points of intersection :g:`b` and :g:`d`, which are also the equinoctial points, and the other passing through both centers, :g:`gh` and :g:`az`, whose tropic points are :g:`h` and :g:`z`.

Since, therefore, our intention is to demonstrate
how much of the equinoctial circle rises in the right sphere with any given degrees of the zodiac, and since the position of the horizon in the right sphere is like that of the meridian circle—namely in the power of straight lines passing through the pole of the equinoctial circle, point :g:`e`, which is the position of the meridian circle—
it is established that since arcs :g:`zb` and :g:`hd` are quadrants of the oblique circle, they rise with arcs :g:`ab` and :g:`gd`, quadrants of the equinoctial circle, and culminate and set equally with them. Now since line :g:`bd` bisects the semidiameter :g:`th` in circle :g:`abgd` orthogonally at point :g:`e`, two equal arcs must be cut off from the zodiac,
namely :g:`bk` and :g:`dl`. Therefore lines :g:`kmen`

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0448
   :page: p. 238

and :g:`lcey` are drawn through.

This done, since parallel circles pass through points :g:`k`, :g:`l` and :g:`y`, :g:`n`, whose distance on either side from the equinoctial circle is equal, so that point :g:`k` is potentially opposite to point :g:`n` and likewise point :g:`l` to point :g:`y`,
if we set arc :g:`bk` as the sign of Pisces, :g:`ld` will be the sign of Libra, and in the same manner :g:`by` in place of Aries and :g:`dn` in place of Virgo.

Therefore, when line :g:`ktl` is drawn through, since triangle :g:`kte` is equal in both sides and angles to triangle :g:`lte`, angle :g:`∠ket` will also be equal to angle :g:`∠let`,
and likewise the remaining angles :g:`∠keb` and :g:`∠led`, and those opposite to them. Since these are at the center of the equinoctial circle, the arcs of that same circle under these angles, which rise with each of these signs, must also be equal; of which it suffices to investigate the quantity of one, say :g:`bm`,
to measure the rising of each.

Accordingly, we drop the perpendicular :g:`tf` onto line :g:`ke`. This done, since out of those parts of which :math:`60^p` contain the semidiameter of the equinoctial circle, :math:`65^p 36' 17''` measure line :g:`tk`, the semidiameter of the zodiac, :math:`26^p 31' 58''` measure line :g:`et` between the centers of the circles,
and :math:`73^p 39' 7''` measure line :g:`ek`, the semidiameter of the parallel circle designated at the beginning of Pisces and the beginning of Scorpio—namely points :g:`k` and :g:`l`—triangle :g:`tke` is known. Therefore, if we apply the square on :g:`tk`, minus the square on :g:`te`, to line :g:`ke`, the excess of line :g:`kf` over line :g:`ef`
will be determined.

For as often as the greater of two mutually intersecting circles bisects the lesser,

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0449
   :page: p. 239

if the square of the distance of the centers be subtracted from the square of the greater semidiameter, there remains the square of the semidiameter of the lesser circle. Here, therefore, since the oblique circle bisects the equinoctial in this manner,
the square on the semidiameter :g:`tk` of the greater exceeds the square on :g:`te`, the distance of the centers, by as much as the semidiameter :g:`eb` of the lesser produces from itself, since angle :g:`∠bet` is right and line :g:`tb` is equal to line :g:`tk`.

Since, moreover, :math:`60^p` parts measure line :g:`eb`, the semidiameter of the equinoctial circle, its square must contain :math:`3600^p` of those same parts, while from the foregoing :math:`73^p 39' 7''` measure line :g:`ek`; and if we apply that difference—namely the square on :g:`eb`—to this line,
there results the excess of line :g:`kf` over line :g:`fe`, which is :math:`48^p 52' 42''`. When this is subtracted from line :g:`ke`, there remain :math:`24^p 46' 25''`, half of which—namely :math:`12^p 23' 12''`—will measure line :g:`fe`, out of those parts of which :math:`26^p 31' 58''` measure line :g:`et`.

Therefore, out of those parts of which there are :math:`120^p` in line :g:`et`, which subtends the right angle :g:`∠eft`, there must be numbered in line :g:`fe` nearly :math:`55^p 59'`, and the arc of chord :g:`fe` must measure :math:`55^\circ 40'` out of the :math:`360^\circ` of the whole circle circumscribing right triangle :g:`fet`.

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0450
   :page: p. 240

Therefore,
out of the :math:`360^\circ` in four right angles, angle :g:`∠fte` will contain :math:`27^\circ 50'`. This angle together with angle :g:`∠fet` equals a right angle, which itself likewise completes a right angle with angle :g:`∠bek`; therefore, subtracting the common intermediate angle, angle :g:`∠bek` remains equal to angle :g:`∠fte`, so :math:`27^\circ 50'` measure angle :g:`∠bek`.
And since this is at the center of the equinoctial circle, arc :g:`bm` subtending it must also measure :math:`27^\circ 50'` out of the :math:`360^\circ` of the whole equinoctial circle. These, therefore, are the degrees and minutes—as laid down in the corporeal sphere—of the equinoctial circle with which the four signs placed around the equinoctial points rise in the position of the fixed sphere.

Yet we can also arrive at this by an easier way. For as :g:`ke` is in :g:`en`, so is :g:`be` in :g:`ed`; now :g:`be` in :g:`ed` is :math:`3600^p`, which when divided by line :g:`ek` yields line :g:`en`, so it is established to be known.
And since :g:`ke` exceeds this by twice line :g:`fe`, it follows that :g:`fe` is likewise known. Moreover :g:`et` is also known; and since it is opposite the right angle at :g:`f`, angle :g:`∠fte`—namely the angle equal to :g:`∠keb`—will be known, from which follows the knowledge of arc :g:`bm`.
