Section 14 · Construction of Parallels to the Zodiac via the Equinoctial
========================================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 14 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 249–251 <heiberg:1907>`)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0459
   :page: p. 249

The preceding part of this treatise covers circles parallel to the right circle as far as the rising of the signs;
its continuation treats parallels to the zodiac, insofar as they determine the places of fixed stars, by which reason the instrument called the spider in the horoscoping instrument contains them. Therefore, from the circles described, we take the outermost one

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0460
   :page: p. 250

containing all others within it,
and describe it with letters :g:`a`, :g:`b`, :g:`g`, :g:`d` around center :g:`e` in place of the meridian circles, its diameters cutting each other orthogonally being :g:`ag` and :g:`bd`. Having done this, we cut off from point :g:`d` arc :g:`dz`, whose quantity is at the measure of the distance from the equinoctial circle
of a circle parallel to it described toward the south pole in the corporeal sphere. Then we draw a line from point :g:`g` parallel to line :g:`ed`, bounded by letters :g:`g` and :g:`h`; likewise a perpendicular :g:`ht` descends from point :g:`h` onto line :g:`ed`, and :g:`g` joins :g:`d`, crossing line :g:`ht` at point :g:`k`.
**I say that** if we cut off from line :g:`eg` a segment equal to :g:`tk` up to point :g:`l`, and around center :g:`e` at radius :g:`el` describe circle :g:`clm`, the distance of :g:`abgd` from circle :g:`clm` will be designated at the measure of an arc similar to arc :g:`dz`.
To make this plain, :g:`g` joins :g:`m`, cutting circle :g:`clm` at point :g:`n`; arc :g:`mn` will be similar to arc :g:`dz`, and so arc :g:`gz`, the remainder of the quadrant of its circle, is similar to arc :g:`ln`, the remainder of the quarter circle;

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0461
   :page: p. 251

which may be shown clearly as follows.
For as :g:`de` is to line :g:`eg`, so is :g:`dt` to line :g:`tk`. But :g:`de` is equal to :g:`eg`; therefore :g:`dt` is also equal to :g:`tk`. Yet :g:`tk` equals :g:`em`; therefore :g:`em` equals :g:`td`. Taking :g:`tm` as a common middle segment, :g:`et` will also equal :g:`md`.
Moreover, :g:`et` was both equal and parallel to :g:`gh`; so therefore :g:`md` is also parallel and equal to the same :g:`gh`; whence :g:`hd` and :g:`gm` must also be equal and parallel. Therefore angle :g:`∠gme` equals angle :g:`∠zde`;
whence it follows that arc :g:`cln` is similar to arc :g:`bgz`, and thus also the remainder to the remainder of the semicircles—that is, :g:`mn` is similar to that which is :g:`zd`. Therefore, if circle :g:`clm` be set as the equinoctial, circle :g:`abgd` will be designated from it at the distance of arc :g:`ln` similar to arc :g:`gz`.
