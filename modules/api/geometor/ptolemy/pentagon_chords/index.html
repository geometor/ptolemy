geometor.ptolemy.pentagon_chords¶

Exact symbolic construction of Ptolemy’s Golden Pentagon-Decagon & Sexagesimal Chords.

Implements Almagest Book I, Ch. 10a (Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 31-36 & 38-46):

  1. Ptolemy’s Golden Pentagon-Decagon Construction (construct_pentagon_decagon()): Constructs both the side of the regular decagon ($s_{10} = DZ = R / \phi$) and the side of the regular pentagon ($s_5 = BZ = R \sqrt{3 - \phi}$) inside a single circle of radius $R$ using geometor.model with auto_intersect=False and zero floating-point coordinates.

  2. Exact Radical Chord Table (exact_chord(), build_radical_chord_table()): Derives exact closed-form algebraic radical expressions in SymPy for Ptolemy’s canonical chord chain ($120^circ, 90^circ, 72^circ, 60^circ, 36^circ, 12^circ, 6^circ, 3^circ, 1frac{1}{2}^circ, frac{3}{4}^circ$) on diameter $2R = 120^p$.

  3. Exact Base-60 Sexagesimal Arithmetic (Sexagesimal, verify_ptolemy_pi()): Converts exact SymPy algebraic and rational numbers into Ptolemy’s sexagesimal notation ($p; m', s''$) and verifies Ptolemy’s Inequality Lemma bounds on $\operatorname{crd}(1^\circ) \approx 1;2,50 = \frac{377}{360}$ and $\pi \approx 3;8,30 = \frac{377}{120}$.

geometor.ptolemy.pentagon_chords.construct_pentagon_decagon(model: geometor.model.Model | None = None, *, radius: int | sympy.Rational = 60) → PentagonDecagonResult¶

Construct the regular decagon side $DZ$ and pentagon side $BZ$ (Almagest I.10, Heiberg I, pp. 32-35).

Follows Ptolemy’s exact Euclidean steps with Greek letter mapping ($\mathrm{A}\to A$, $\mathrm{B}\to B$, $\mathrm{\Gamma}\to C$, $\mathrm{\Delta}\to D$, $\mathrm{E}\to E$, $\mathrm{Z}\to Z$):

  • Given: Circle $ABC$ on diameter $ADC$ centered at $D = (0, 0)$ with radius $R$, and perpendicular radius $DB \perp AC$.

  • Construct: Bisect $DC$ at $E$ (Eucl. I.10); join $EB$; describe circle $(E, B)$ meeting $AD$ at $Z$ so $EZ = EB$; join $ZB$.

  • Demonstrate:

    1. $CZ \cdot ZD + DE^2 = EZ^2 = EB^2 = ED^2 + DB^2 \implies CZ \cdot ZD = DB^2 = DC^2$ (Eucl. II.6, I.47), so $ZC$ is cut in extreme and mean ratio at $D$ and $DZ = R / \phi = s_{10}$ (Eucl. VI.Def.3, XIII.9).

    2. $Q(D, B) + Q(D, Z) = Q(B, Z)$ (Eucl. I.47), so $s_6^2 + s_{10}^2 = BZ^2$ and $BZ = s_5$ is the side of the inscribed regular pentagon (Eucl. XIII.10).

Parameters:

  • model – Optional target Model (created with auto_intersect=False if None).

  • radius – Exact rational circle radius $R$ (defaults to Ptolemy’s $R = 60$).

Returns:

Verified PentagonDecagonResult over $\mathbb{Q}(\phi)$.

geometor.ptolemy.pentagon_chords.verify_ptolemy_pi() → PtolemyPiVerification¶

Verify Ptolemy’s Inequality Lemma (Almagest I.10, Heiberg I, pp. 43-46) & $\pi \approx 3;8,30$.

Ptolemy proves geometrically that for $0 < \beta < \alpha < 180^\circ$:

$$\frac{\operatorname{crd}(\alpha)}{\operatorname{crd}(\beta)} < \frac{\alpha}{\beta}$$

Applying this twice to $(\frac{3}{4}^\circ, 1^\circ)$ and $(1^\circ, 1\frac{1}{2}^\circ)$ yields:

$$\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{crd}(1\tfrac{1}{2}^\circ) < \operatorname{crd}(1^\circ) < \frac{4}{3}\operatorname{crd}(\tfrac{3}{4}^\circ)$$

Both bounds round in two sexagesimal places to $1;2,50 = \frac{377}{360}$, implying circumference / diameter $approx frac{360 cdot (1;2,50)}{120} = 3 cdot (1;2,50) = 3;8,30 = frac{377}{120} approx 3.1416667$ (Almagest VI.7, Heiberg I, p. 513).
