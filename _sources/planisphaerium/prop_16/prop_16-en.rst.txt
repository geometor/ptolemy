Section 16 · Drawing a Parallel to the Zodiac in the Planisphere
================================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 16 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 252–254 <heiberg:1907>`)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1907_opera_minora_vol2:0462
   :page: p. 252

Now the description of parallels to the zodiac in the planisphere must be set forth. Therefore we describe the meridian circle passing through both poles :g:`abgd` around center :g:`e`, understanding line :g:`deb` as the intelligible axis, point :g:`d` as the south pole, :g:`aeg` as the diameter of the equinoctial circle,
and :g:`zht` as the diameter of the circle parallel to the zodiac which we propose to describe in the planisphere. We therefore draw through point :g:`h` a line parallel to line :g:`ag` bounded by letters :g:`k` and :g:`l`, joining lines :g:`dmz`, :g:`dcl`, and :g:`dnt`.
**I say that** the circle whose diameter is :g:`zt` can be designated about diameter :g:`mn`. Its proof: for it will touch on both sides the two circles parallel to the equinoctial whose distance from it is in the quantity of arcs :g:`az` and :g:`gt`; and therefore we describe those two circles with the two lengths :g:`em` and :g:`en`;
and it will also bisect the circle parallel to the equinoctial whose diameter is :g:`lk`

at the meridian circle whose diameter is :g:`bd`, which we describe at the length :g:`ce` between points :g:`c`, :g:`y`, and :g:`f`, which the circle described about :g:`mn` will bisect, passing through points :g:`y` and :g:`f`,
and will divide circle :g:`cy` into two halves, just as happens in the corporeal sphere. Therefore straight lines will join :g:`b` with :g:`z` and :g:`b` with :g:`q`; and :g:`kl` and :g:`dt` will go straight forward until they meet at point :g:`r`, which lastly will join with :g:`b`.
Since therefore the two angles :g:`∠bzd` and :g:`∠bhq` are right, it follows that points :g:`b`, :g:`h`, :g:`z`, :g:`q` lie upon the circumference of a circle; whence angle :g:`∠bqh` must equal angle :g:`∠bzh`, which equals angle :g:`∠bdt`, since their bases are the same;
thus angle :g:`∠bqr` equals angle :g:`∠bdr`, whence it is clear that points :g:`b`, :g:`d`, :g:`r`, :g:`q` lie upon the circumference of a circle. Therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`bh` and :g:`hd` equals that contained by :g:`rh` and :g:`hq`. But :g:`bh` by :g:`hd` equals the square on :g:`hl`;
therefore the square on :g:`hl` equals

the rectangle contained by :g:`rh` and :g:`hq`. Moreover, :g:`rq` is parallel to line :g:`nm`; therefore the rectangle contained by :g:`em` and :g:`en` equals the square on :g:`ec`. And since :g:`ec` equals lines :g:`ey` and :g:`ef`, it follows that points :g:`n`, :g:`y`, :g:`m`, :g:`f` lie upon the circumference of a circle.
