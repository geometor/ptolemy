Section 4 · Circular Inversion & Astrolabe Radii from the Almagest
==================================================================

*Planisphaerium*, Section 4 (:cite:`Heiberg 1907, Vol. II, pp. 232–235 <heiberg:1907>`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. picture:: /_static/figures/fig09_planisphaerium_stereographic_astrolabe.svg

Theorem on Inverse Proportionality of Parallels (`Heiberg II, pp. 232–233`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

:line:`p. 232` These things having been established, we must now consider the ratio of the semidiameters of the parallel circles designated above the signs of the oblique circle to the semidiameter of the right circle, until we apprehend their rising and measure them with a definite number, as appears in the corporeal sphere.

:line:`p. 233` Let the equinoctial circle :g:`abgd <cABGD>` be described around center :g:`e <E>`, whose diameters cutting each other orthogonally are :g:`ag <MK>` and :g:`db <BD_ast>`. We extend :g:`ag <MK>` straight to point :g:`z`, and around :g:`g <K>` cut off two equal arcs :g:`gt` and :g:`gh`. Straight lines :g:`dkh` and :g:`dtz` are drawn, so that the northern parallel is made around center :g:`e <E>` at radius :g:`eh` and the southern parallel around the same center at radius :g:`ez`.

**I say that as** :g:`ez` **is to** :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, **so is** :g:`ed <BD_ast>` **to** :g:`eh`:

$$\frac{ez}{ed} = \frac{ed}{eh}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
ez \cdot eh = ed^2 = R_{\text{eq}}^2.$$

*Demonstration.* Since arc :g:`gh` and arc :g:`gt` are equal, arcs :g:`bt` and :g:`bh` together equal a semicircle ($180^\circ$); whence it follows that angles :g:`∠bdt` and :g:`∠bdh` together equal a right angle ($90^\circ$).

Moreover, in right triangle :g:`edh`, angles :g:`∠edh` and :g:`∠ehd` together equal a right angle. Therefore the two right-angled triangles :g:`edh` and :g:`edz` are similar!

Whence it is necessary that as :g:`ez` is to :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, so is :g:`ed <BD_ast>` to :g:`eh`.

Numerical Radii Derived from the *Almagest* (`Heiberg II, pp. 234–235`)
^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^^

:line:`p. 234` These things being held, we measure first each of arcs :g:`gh` and :g:`gt` as $23^\circ 51' 20''$ out of the $360^\circ$ measuring the right circle, which is the distance of each tropic from the equinoctial on the solid sphere.

According to this quantity of distance:

* Arc :g:`bt` $= 90^\circ + 23^\circ 51' 20'' = 113^\circ 51' 20''$.
* Arc :g:`bh` (residue to a semicircle) $= 180^\circ - 113^\circ 51' 20'' = 66^\circ 8' 40''$.
* Chord of arc :g:`bt` $= 100;33,28^p$ out of $120^p$ for the diameter, **as was established in the Almagest** (*quemadmodum in Almagesti constitutum est*).
* Chord of arc :g:`bh` $= 65;29,0^p$.

Therefore the ratio of $100;33,28^p$ to $65;29,0^p$ is the ratio of line :g:`ez` to line :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, and of :g:`ed <BD_ast>` to line :g:`eh`.

Since therefore :g:`ed <BD_ast>`, the semidiameter of the right circle, is set absolutely to $60^p$:

1. **Winter Tropic (Capricorn) Semidiameter**:

   $$r_{\text{Cap}} = ez = 92;8,15^p \qquad (\text{Latin: }\textit{partibus XCII puncta VIII secunde XV}).$$

2. **Summer Tropic (Cancer) Semidiameter**:

   $$r_{\text{Can}} = eh = 39;4,19^p \qquad (\text{Latin: }\textit{partes XXXIX puncta IIII secunde XVIIII}).$$

:line:`p. 235` 3. **Total Ecliptic Diameter & Center Offset**:

   - Total Diameter ($ez + eh$): $131;12,34^p$.
   - Semidiameter of the Zodiac: $65;36,17^p$.
   - Distance of Zodiac Center from Equinoctial Center :g:`e <E>`:

     $$d_{\text{ecl}} = 65;36,17^p - 39;4,19^p = 26;31,58^p.$$
