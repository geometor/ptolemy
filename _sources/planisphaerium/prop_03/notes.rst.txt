Section 3 · Notes
=================

Euclidean Power Theorems in Stereographic Projection
----------------------------------------------------

1. **Euclid III.35 as an Inversive Invariant**:
   Ptolemy's reliance on :just:`Eucl. III.35 <III.35>` (:math:`ae \cdot eg = he \cdot et`) illustrates how classical Greek geometers handled the **power of a point with respect to a circle**. In the modern theory of circle inversion (developed by Steiner, Möbius, and Plücker in the 19th century), this property is expressed by stating that the point :math:`e` has constant power :math:`-R^2` with respect to every circle representing an inclined great circle on the sphere.

2. **Astrolabe Rete and Plate Interaction**:
   In the physical astrolabe, the zodiac circle is engraved on the rotating pierced metal plate called the **Rete** (*al-`Ankabūt*, the Spider), while the horizon circles are engraved on the stationary plate beneath (*tympan*). Ptolemy's proof in Section 3 guarantees that as the user rotates the Rete around the central pin (North Pole :math:`e`), the rising and setting ecliptic degrees marked by the horizon circle always correspond to exactly six zodiacal signs (:math:`180^\circ` apart), accurately tracking the astronomical phenomena of rising and setting.
