Chapter 14 · Summary
====================

Derivation of the Solar Declination Law
---------------------------------------

Chapter 14 (*Heiberg I, pp. 76–79*) gives the first spherical astronomical application of the Menelaus Theorem (Chapter 13), determining the meridian arc of declination :math:`\delta = H\Theta` between the celestial equator and the ecliptic as a function of ecliptic longitude :math:`\lambda = EH`:

.. math::

   \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 ZA)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 AB)} = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Theta Z)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 \Theta H)} \cdot \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2 HE)}{\operatorname{crd}(2 EB)}
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   \operatorname{crd}(2\delta) = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2\varepsilon)\,\operatorname{crd}(2\lambda)}{120}.

* **Standard Conventions**: Great-circle arcs are measured in :math:`360^\circ` and chords in parts where the diameter is :math:`120^p`.
* **Worked Examples**:
  - For :math:`\lambda = 30^\circ`: :math:`\operatorname{crd}(2\delta) = 24^p 15' 56''`, giving :math:`2\delta = 23^\circ 19' 59''` and :math:`\delta \approx 11^\circ 40'` (tabulated as :math:`11^\circ 39' 59''` in Chapter 15).
  - For :math:`\lambda = 60^\circ`: :math:`\operatorname{crd}(2\delta) = 42^p 1' 48''`, giving :math:`2\delta = 41^\circ 0' 18''` and :math:`\delta = 20^\circ 30' 9''`.
