Chapter 6 · That the Earth Has the Ratio of a Point to the Heavens
==================================================================

*Almagest* Book I, Chapter 6 (:cite:`Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 20–21 <heiberg:1898>`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0030
   :page: p. 20

Moreover, that the earth also has the ratio of a point to sense perception with respect to the distance to the sphere of the so-called fixed stars, a great proof is that from all parts of it the sizes and distances of the stars at the same times appear equal and similar everywhere,
just as observations of the same stars from different climes are not found to disagree in the least. Indeed, this too must be added, that gnomons set up in any part of the earth, and likewise the centers of armillary spheres, have the same effect as the true center of the earth,
and preserve the sightings and the revolutions of shadows so completely in agreement with the hypotheses of the phenomena as if they took place through the middle point of the earth itself.

And a manifest sign that these things are so is also that everywhere the planes projected through our lines of sight, which we call horizons,
always bisect the whole sphere of the heavens, which would not happen if the

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0031
   :page: p. 21

size of the earth were perceptible relative to the distance of the heavens;
rather, only the plane projected through the point at the center of the earth would be able to bisect the sphere, while those through any surface of the earth whatever would always make the sections below the earth greater than those above the earth.
