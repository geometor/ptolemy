Chapter 15 · Summary
====================

The 90-Degree Solar Declination Table
-------------------------------------

Chapter 15 (*Heiberg I, pp. 80–81*) presents the complete quadrant table mapping ecliptic longitude :math:`\lambda \in \{1^\circ, 2^\circ, \dots, 90^\circ\}` (measured from the vernal or autumnal equinox) to the corresponding meridian arc of solar declination :math:`\delta(\lambda)` between the celestial equator and the ecliptic:

.. math::

   \operatorname{crd}(2\delta) = \frac{\operatorname{crd}(2\varepsilon)\,\operatorname{crd}(2\lambda)}{120}, \qquad \varepsilon = 23^\circ 51' 20''.

* **Quadrant Symmetry**: Because the four quadrants of the ecliptic are symmetric about the equinoxes and solstices, the 90 entries suffice for the entire :math:`360^\circ` zodiac.
* **Key Checkpoints**: At :math:`\lambda = 30^\circ` (end of Aries), :math:`\delta = 11^\circ 39' 59''`; at :math:`\lambda = 60^\circ` (end of Taurus), :math:`\delta = 20^\circ 30' 9''`; and at :math:`\lambda = 90^\circ` (solstice), :math:`\delta = \varepsilon = 23^\circ 51' 20''`.
