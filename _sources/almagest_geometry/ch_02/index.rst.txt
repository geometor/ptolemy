Chapter 2 · On the Order of the Theorems
========================================

*Almagest* Book I, Chapter 2 (:cite:`Heiberg 1898, Vol. I.1, pp. 8–10 <heiberg:1898>`)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0018
   :page: p. 8

First, then, in our present treatise comes seeing the general relation of the whole earth to the whole heaven; and of the particular topics in sequence, the first would be to go through the account concerning the position of the oblique circle
and the places of our inhabited world, and furthermore the difference arising among them with respect to each horizon owing to the inclinations

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0019
   :page: p. 9

in their arrangements; for the theory of these, grasped beforehand, makes the examination of the rest easier.
Second, to go through the motion of the sun and of the moon and their accompanying phenomena; for without grasping these beforehand it would not be possible to examine the matters concerning the stars in detail.
And since the account of the stars comes last in our method, here too the account of the sphere of the so-called fixed stars would fittingly be placed first, and that of the five called planets would follow.
Each of these we shall try to show using as starting points and as it were foundations for discovery the clear phenomena and the indisputable observations of both the ancients and our own time, fitting the subsequent conclusions together by means of demonstrations in the geometrical methods.

Now the general outline to assume beforehand would be of this sort: that the heaven is spherical and moves spherically, and that the earth too in figure is spherical to sense perception when taken as a whole, and in position lies in the middle of the whole heaven just like a center,
and in size and distance has the ratio of a point to the sphere of the fixed stars, itself making no local

.. folio:: heiberg_1898_syntaxis_vol1_pt1:0020
   :page: p. 10

motion. We shall go through each of these briefly by way of reminder.
